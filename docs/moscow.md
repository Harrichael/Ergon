# MoSCoW prioritization

## Goal

This master list of MoSCoW classification of our work will give us:

- a) Clarity of priorities and scope until launch
- b) A well defined Minimal Shippable Product
  - i) that is Excellent, and
  - ii) that we are uber confident will be done BEFORE launch
- c) Every line item or idea, with regard to this project, has a clear home or path forward

If this document is successful, you will be able to:

- a) know where any Jira ticket or loose thread you have fits under the 4 MoSCoW buckets
- b) effectively prioritize your work and path forward, and
- c) see a collective path to success for the product

## The buckets

| MoSCoW | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| [Must Have](#must-have) | Minimal Shippable Product. |
| [Should Have](#should-have) | with a Capital S. We are committed to this work, but it doesn't put Launch at risk. |
| [Could Have](#could-have) | Maybe, but unclear. |
| [Won't Have](#wont-have) | Not now, but maybe later. Out of Scope. |

### Must Have

Minimal Shippable Product.

We can ship this and users/stakeholders will be happy, though we may even HIGH priority follow-ons. While it satisfies security requirements and protects the business's image (including excellence and polish), it may contain

- a) manual work in a backroom for certain processes,
- b) have temporary scaffolding that will be obviated in later phases, and
- c) significant scope reduction that may even be user facing.

The requirement to put something here is strict.

- a) If line item was missing, would we delay launch?
- b) Are we okay building the line item and NOT a working product by the due date?

### Should Have

with a Capital S. We are committed to this work, but it doesn't put Launch at risk.

Things here should be:

- a) unambiguous and well defined
- b) about de-risking product launch to ensure success

Not disqualifying:

- a) may be required for long term product success. Can we get it done after launch before it risks project failure?
- b) user facing features
- c) critical architectural / non-functional requirements

What this is not for:

- a) wish list
- b) work that is primarily about other projects. That can be tackled and prioritized with those projects.

### Could Have

Maybe, but unclear.

There is significant ambiguity - either around requirements, scope, product market fit, user expectations, business needs, capacity, or technical implementation - that makes this bucket "uncommitted". However, these line items will:

- a) be evaluated AFTER launch
- b) explicitly accepted OR rejected

### Won't Have

Not now, but maybe later. Out of Scope.

Requires future projects, efforts, or product iteration cycles to be picked up.

Suggestions only; nothing above has been changed.

- **Must Have, first paragraph:** "though we may even HIGH priority follow-ons" is missing a verb. Perhaps "though we may even have HIGH priority follow-ons".
- **Must Have, list a–c:** the items don't share one grammar. "it may contain ... b) have temporary scaffolding" reads as "it may contain have". Either drop "have" from b, or lead in with "it may" and start each item with a verb ("a) contain manual work ... b) have temporary scaffolding ... c) include significant scope reduction").
- **Must Have, strict test a:** "If line item was missing" reads more smoothly as "If this line item were missing".
- **Must Have, strict test b:** this question may read backwards. As written, answering "yes" sounds like a reason to include the item. If the intent is "Would we rather build this item than have a working product by the due date?", consider rewording.
- **Should Have, opening:** "with a Capital S" starts mid-sentence. Perhaps "Should, with a capital S." Also "Capital" and "Launch" don't need capitals.
- **Should Have, "Not disqualifying":** an example of the long-term-success test would help, since it's the subtlest call in the doc.
- **Could Have, item b:** "explicitly accepted OR rejected" needs "be" to match a: "be explicitly accepted OR rejected".
- **Could Have:** the dashes around the list of ambiguities would read better as em dashes (—).
- **Goal, item c:** "has a clear home" doesn't follow "will give us" like a and b do. Perhaps "c) A clear home or path forward for every line item or idea in this project".
- **Hyphens:** "well-defined", "long-term", "user-facing", "product-market fit" are hyphenated when they come before a noun.
- **Won't Have:** consider saying where these items get recorded, so "maybe later" has somewhere to come back from.
